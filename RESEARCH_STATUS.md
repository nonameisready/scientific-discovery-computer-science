## 2026-10-16: CS3 corridor-signature stress test — equivalent reformulation, no-go as an intermediate

The proposed per-LCA-guard `O(r^3)` signature claim is equivalent up to constants to the full simple-treewidth-3 profile conjecture. In the source-standard construction, guards are bags/unions of bags in a width-3 decomposition of the measured graph `G` (obtainable by intersecting a host simple-3-tree decomposition with `V(G)`); each guard has at most eight actual `G` vertices. Thus each local signature is itself a truncated profile on at most eight landmarks. Conversely, the source LCA family has at most `4|A|` guards, and a valid guard's distances in `G` determine the truncated profile to `A`. The complete two-way proof is in [the stress-test report](reports/simple_treewidth3_CS3_stress_test_20261016.md).

The cited treewidth-3 `Ω(r^4)` lower construction is still ineligible: its `K3,3` minor prevents it from being planar/simple-tw3. No eligible asymptotic counterexample or proof was found. CS3 is closed as an independent intermediate direction; no successor was enqueued, no experiment ran, and Paper 3/4 were unchanged. ## 2026-10-16: simple-treewidth-3 profile complexity — bounded primary-source reconstruction

The primary-source reconstruction confirms the explicit conjecture `pc_r(G,k)=O_t(r^t k)` for graphs of simple treewidth at most `t`; at `t=3` the target is `O(r^3 k)`. Beaudou et al.'s current text proves `O_t(r^(t+1)k)` for ordinary treewidth, while outerplanar graphs have `pc_r(G,A)<=1+(2r+2)^2|A|` by a circular BFS-layer monotonicity argument. The extra `r` in the general bound enters in the minor-free profile count for each constant-size LCA guard, not in the `O(k)` number of guards.

The Joret–Rambaud `t=3` general-treewidth lower construction does **not** refute the simple-treewidth target: a direct branch-set audit gives it a `K_{3,3}` minor, whereas every simple 3-tree is planar. A size-3 guard, if an `O(k)` family existed and hit every global short path, would indeed determine each truncated profile; its existence remains unresolved, not refuted. One restricted lemma was proved: maximal simple 3-tree hosts themselves satisfy `pc_r(H,A)<=4|A|(16(r+1)+1)^2` by clique-bag LCA guards and bounded within-clique distance signatures. This does not transfer to proper subgraphs, because deleting host edges changes distances (e.g. `P4` versus its `K4` completion).

**Disposition:** no theorem for all simple-treewidth-3 subgraphs, no asymptotic counterexample, and no publication-level result established. The exact remaining obligation is an `O(r^3)` joint partial-graph distance-signature bound per standard LCA guard (especially two-sided corridors), or a source-eligible asymptotic counterexample. This bounded cycle is closed; no successor task was created. ## 2026-10-16: caterpillar extra-traversal accounting and DAG novelty audit

**Exact identity proved:** For every token `t` and edge `e` on a tree, `N_t(e)=1[e in P_t]+2b_t(e)` for a unique integer `b_t(e)>=0`, by cut-side parity. If `s(e)` counts swaps and `r(e)=C(e)/2`, then `sum_t b_t(e)=s(e)-r(e)`. This counts extra traversals per token; sharing an edge with both original paths does not make a particular swap “mandatory.”

**Novelty correction:** A choice of opposite-direction pairings with acyclic token-event precedence graph exists iff a sequential solution has exactly `L/2` swaps, where `L` is total initial token distance. This follows from Miltzow et al.'s distance lower bound (ESA 2016, Lemma 2) and the exact equality criterion of Bonnet–Miltzow–Rzążewski (Algorithmica 2018, Proposition 2), which says equality holds iff a happy-swap-only solution exists. The DAG is an identity-level representation of that criterion, not a new routing theorem. Akers–Krishnamurthy's tree method includes happy swaps and shoves; Biniaz et al. explain that optimal tree sequences can move a correctly placed leaf. These are sequential-swap facts, not parallel matching-round makespan bounds.

## 2026-10-16: parallel token swapping theory checkpoint

The queued C+D schedule conjecture is **disproved** on arbitrary trees. For every (m\ge2), an (m)-leaf star with the center token fixed and leaf tokens in one cycle has (D=2), (C(e)=2) on every edge, but exact (operatorname{OPT}=m+1). The lower bound (m+1) follows because all star edges share the center and the fixed center token must leave and return; the schedule (e_0,e_1,\ldots,e_{m-1},e_0) is exact. Thus no universal additive constant can repair (C+D).

The originally stated (operatorname{OPT}\ge\max(C,D)) is also corrected: a two-vertex exchange has (C=2,D=1,operatorname{OPT}=1). The valid lower bound is
(LB_0=\max(D,\max_v\frac12\sum_{e\ni v}C(e))), with ceilings for integer rounds. A further fixed-hub lemma gives (q(v)+1) when the token initially and finally at (v) is fixed and (q(v)) incident edges carry demand. This exactly explains the star's extra round.

The theory cycle did not prove or refute a constant-factor polynomial schedule relative to (LB_0) or the hub-strengthened bound. Static congestion slots do not preserve token identities or occupied-vertex dependencies. The narrow surviving obligation is a dependency-aware lower bound plus a token-preserving exchange lemma. The result is a substantive correction and a bounded theoretical advance, not a new approximation theorem.
