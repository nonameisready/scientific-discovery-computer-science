\begin{abstract}
We ask which specific computer-science questions are strongest candidates for substantive technical progress in 2027--2028 and 2029--2031. We define progress as a verified theorem or bound, matched-baseline algorithmic improvement, independent replication, independently checked counterexample, justified premise revision, or material narrowing of a frozen question---not attention or publication counts. We audit ten frozen prospective events without altering them, reassess a twelve-item CS radar, and screen new primary-source leads in formal verification, AI inference, agent verification, databases, graph algorithms, and cryptography. The resulting two-horizon portfolio is a qualitative research judgment, not a calibrated probability model. Near-term leaders are repository-scale code-and-proof synthesis, constrained LLM inference scheduling, intermediate-regime adaptive privacy filters, dynamic approximate matching, and artifact-level agent verification. Medium-horizon candidates include the dynamic matching--ordered-Ruzsa--Szemer\'edi bottleneck, planar Three-Sets Cut-Uncut, planar discrete Fr\'echet under translation, two-stage integer programming with unrestricted global coefficients, and reproducible scientific agents. Recent primary sources show concrete progress, but those results are author-reported and not independently reproduced here. Paper~3 has no validated predictive performance on CS Target~A or B; Paper~4 provides a transparent action-structuring vocabulary but no demonstrated planner superiority. This is a falsifiable qualitative portfolio, not evidence that AI can predict breakthroughs.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} computer science; research forecasting; evidence adjudication; formal verification; inference systems; dynamic algorithms.

\section{Introduction}
Forecasting research progress is vulnerable to hindsight, vague outcomes, and popularity substitution. A forecast can appear prescient if success is redefined after the event; citation growth is not a theorem; and a search miss is not a negative. We ask which technically precise CS problem families deserve the strongest qualitative expectation of independently verifiable progress over the next five years, and what evidence would change the ordering.

The evidence boundary is 2026-10-17 03:50:01 UTC. Ten existing prospective event records remain immutable commitments, not validated model scores. Their selected, heterogeneous design is not representative of CS. This portfolio is not a population-level estimate.

\section{Related work and originality}
Science-of-science research has predicted citations, impact, or bibliometric breakthrough proxies \citep{min2021breakthrough}. PreScience provides a large time-snapshotted scientific forecasting benchmark over AI papers and future paper/topic outcomes \citep{ajith2026prescience}. ResearchBench scores inspiration retrieval, hypothesis composition, and ranking \citep{liu2026researchbench}; PaperBench evaluates replication of published AI research \citep{starace2025paperbench}. The same project has an astronomy historical-backtesting protocol and a 200-question prospective instance, including an outcome-label agreement study \citep{mao2026questiondiscovery,mao2026backtesting}.

These works rule out a broad ``first scientific forecasting benchmark'' claim. This study contributes an evidence-linked, two-horizon qualitative portfolio with explicit outcome separation and counterevidence. Independent novelty and publication value are not established. A future paper needs a demonstrated methodological increment beyond existing benchmarks, external users, transparent selection, reliable adjudication, and matured outcomes.

\section{Research question and targets}
The primary target is substantial independently verifiable progress (Target~B): a verified theorem/bound, matched-baseline algorithmic improvement, independently replicated empirical improvement, independent counterexample, premise revision, or substantive narrowing. Target~A is direct proof/refutation of the frozen proposition. Attention and real-world impact remain distinct. Contradictory evidence, continued uncertainty, and unadjudicated status remain separate; search non-detection is missing evidence. The separately versioned CS Target~B rubric does not rewrite Paper~3's source-native taxonomy.

\section{Evidence and candidate selection}
We inspected ten frozen events, twelve earlier radar candidates, and seven additional primary-source leads. Related questions are grouped: dynamic matching and ordered Ruzsa--Szemer\'edi (ORS) structure are not independent. New leads included Vero and CoCo-Prover, Spexis, MAS-ProVe, semiring incremental view maintenance, and dynamic maximal matching. Already resolved or endpoint-unstable leads were not promoted. No new event was frozen because the additional leads overlap existing families or lack a non-arbitrary, independently adjudicable threshold. Original event records were unchanged.

\section{Forecasting method}
A candidate passes a source gate only if a primary-source baseline, exact progress object, obstacle, contrary evidence, and independent verification route are identifiable. Eligible questions are ordered qualitatively by technical path, horizon tractability, significance, evidence/endpoint clarity, and counterevidence. No numerical weights or probabilities are used. `OBSERVED\_SOURCE\_FACT` describes dated source statements; `SCIENTIFIC\_HYPOTHESIS` denotes an untested predictive relation; `UNVALIDATED\_HEURISTIC` denotes the ordering; no CS feature here is an `EMPIRICALLY\_SUPPORTED\_PREDICTOR`. Recent progress is not itself a validated predictor of future progress.

\section{Prospective portfolio and results}
For 2027--2028 the ordering is: (1) repository-scale code-and-proof synthesis; (2) inference scheduling jointly handling KV state and speculation; (3) fully adaptive privacy filters in intermediate sampling regimes; (4) fixed-exponent sublinear update time for approximate dynamic maximum matching; and (5) artifact-level multi-agent process verification.

For 2029--2031 the ordering is: (1) the dynamic matching--ORS extremal chain; (2) planar (1,1,2)-terminal Three-Sets Cut-Uncut; (3) exact planar discrete Fr\'echet under translation near the conditional exponent boundary; (4) two-stage IP optimization with unrestricted global matrices under the frozen parameterization; and (5) research agents that execute and independently reproduce evidence. These are relative qualitative priorities, not probabilities or ``more-likely-than-not'' claims.

\section{Technical case studies}
\subsection{Repository-level verified software}
Vero has 43 multi-module instances; its authors report 27 full code-and-proof solves for the strongest evaluated configuration and ten instances resistant to all configurations \citep{ye2026vero}. VeriSoftBench reports difficulty on deep repository dependencies \citep{xin2026verisoft}. CoCo-Prover reports improved success-cost performance, including up to 26/43 Vero solves, in its own evaluation \citep{yao2026cocoprover}. These results support an expectation of benchmark progress, not general correctness. A proof certifies the formal specification; correspondence to intended behavior remains a separate obligation.

\subsection{Inference serving}
KV-constrained scheduling has different guarantees for stochastic and adversarial arrivals; one fully adversarial model admits no constant competitive ratio \citep{jaillet2025kv}. Spexis introduces speculative-parallel scheduling and reports up to 34\% higher request rate on evaluated configurations \citep{jung2026spexis}. A meaningful next test holds memory, quality, arrival trace, hardware, and tail SLO fixed. The reported maximum is neither a universal gain nor an independent replication.

\subsection{Adaptive privacy}
Tran et al. show a natural fully adaptive $f$-DP filter is invalid and construct approximate GDP filters in low and high sampling-rate regimes \citep{tran2026fdp}. The intermediate regime is a specific opportunity, but no general solution or impossibility is assumed. The frozen event's comparator remains flagged for review.

\subsection{Dynamic matching and ORS}
Approximate dynamic maximum-matching bounds are linked to an ordered-RS extremal parameter \citep{assadi2025ors}. Bernstein et al. obtain sublinear update time for dynamic \emph{maximal} matching \citep{bernstein2025maximal}; this is adjacent progress, not the approximate \emph{maximum}-matching result. The ORS event's frozen date conflict is preserved, and the two questions form one family.

\subsection{Agent verification and scientific discovery}
MAS-ProVe reports that process verification does not consistently improve multi-agent outcomes and may have high variance \citep{venkataramani2026masprove}. EIG and ResearchBench evaluate ideation; PaperBench evaluates replication. The astronomy backtesting study reports low agreement between two careful human annotators under its taxonomy \citep{mao2026backtesting}. These facts motivate reliable artifact outcomes, not a claim that autonomous scientists already produce verified discoveries.

\section{Uncertainty and sensitivity}
Proof-tractability emphasis raises adaptive privacy, planar cut-uncut, and two-stage IP. Capability-impact emphasis raises inference and repository code/proof. Evidence-maturity emphasis raises exact theorem endpoints and lowers process verification/autonomous science. Leading-family membership is more robust than rank; medium-horizon order is especially sensitive. These are ordinal scenario checks, not statistical intervals. Selection bias, publication delay, source provenance, benchmark drift, family dependence, and adjudicator reliability remain important uncertainties.

\section{Paper 3 and Paper 4 integration}
Paper~3's astronomy attention AUC is not a CS Target~A/B result. Historical CS outcomes do not meet sample, class, group, and adjudication gates. No CS model was fitted or scored, and no AUC, calibration, or probability is reported. Paper~4 helps structure competing explanations, provenance-linked gaps, and evidence actions. Expected information gain is not assigned numerically, and no action-efficacy study demonstrates planner superiority.

\section{Evaluation, adjudication, and limitations}
Future Target~A and Target~B evaluations should preserve immutable event text, cutoffs, question-family groups, and chronological splits. Independent human reviewers must compare primary-source scope; disagreement and missingness should be reported. Missing evidence is not a negative. Model comparisons require eligible outcomes, untouched future data, simple source-only baselines, and calibration checks. Paper~4 requires prospective action rankings followed by independent feasibility and evidence-yield coding.

The current portfolio is a single researcher's purposive shortlist. No independent panel reviewed it. No calibrated base rate exists for the selected event classes. Source hashes do not cover cited full-text bytes; author-reported results are not independent replications; event definitions and horizons differ. This study establishes no benchmark novelty, predictive accuracy, planner efficacy, or new CS theorem.

\section{Conclusion}
The strongest qualitative near-term opportunities are repository-scale verified code/proof synthesis and constrained inference scheduling; medium-term candidates include dynamic matching/ORS and specific planar and fine-grained algorithmic problems. Adaptive privacy is a sharply defined theory target. Process verification and evidence-generating research agents remain highly uncertain. These judgments include explicit counterevidence and are uncalibrated. Paper~3 has no validated CS predictive power and Paper~4 has no demonstrated superiority. This is an internal research manuscript, not submission-ready.
