# Publication plan: two-stage prospective CS forecasting program

## Stage 1 — benchmark and methodology (current)

**Working title:** *Prospective Forecasting of Computer Science Research Breakthroughs: An Evidence-Grounded Benchmark and Evaluation Framework*.

**Claim ceiling:** a proposed protocol and small initial prospective cohort. The paper can explain how to define forecastable scientific events, freeze source cutoffs, represent dependent research families, document exclusions, preserve censoring, and adjudicate outcomes. It cannot claim validated AI forecasting ability or benchmark readiness at scale.

**Evidence needed before submission:** independent event review; prior-art review; complete inclusion/exclusion flow; formal Target B rubric; two-reviewer evidence adjudication pilot; schema/data dictionary/license audit; provenance and reproducibility tests; clear treatment of the 2026-10-17 issue-time boundary. The cohort currently has 10 qualitative questions and no outcomes; that is a pilot limitation, not evidence of skill.

**Possible venue fit (not a submission decision):** [*Quantitative Science Studies*](https://direct.mit.edu/qss) is directly scoped to quantitative/theoretical/empirical study of science and its evaluation; [*Research Evaluation*](https://academic.oup.com/rev/pages/About) is suitable if the central contribution is framed as research-evaluation methodology. A machine-learning datasets/evaluation venue could be considered only after the cohort is larger and the current call requirements and deadlines are checked. The [NeurIPS 2026 Evaluations & Datasets call](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/CallForEvaluationsDatasets) is already closed as of this plan; no submission or contact is authorized or implied.

## Stage 2 — predictive validity (future)

**Question:** can models forecast Target A direct resolution/refutation and Target B substantial independently verifiable progress, separately, and outperform simple point-in-time baselines?

This stage must wait until enough events mature and two independent human reviewers adjudicate them. Required design: frozen chronological train/validation/test cutoffs; research-family grouping; no post-cutoff features; publication-volume and field-momentum baselines; source-only text baseline; probability forecasts only if a model is genuinely calibrated on training data; proper scoring/calibration metrics with cluster-aware uncertainty; report class counts and power; no missing-as-negative imputation. Target A and Target B require distinct labels and analyses.

## Governance and stop rules

No outcome crawling or monitoring is active in v2. Activation requires separate authorization and a source-coverage plan. A search miss does not imply nonoccurrence. Do not publish to OpenFindings or submit to a venue without explicit authorization. If event definitions prove incomparable or label counts remain inadequate, report no-score rather than manufacture an evaluation.
