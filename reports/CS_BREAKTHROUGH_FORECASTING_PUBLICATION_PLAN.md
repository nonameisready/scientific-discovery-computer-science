# Publication plan: two-stage prospective CS forecasting program

**Updated:** 2026-10-17 after the 10.0 prior-art, source, and manuscript audit. This is a planning note only; it is not a submission authorization.

## Current decision

The prior two-stage idea remains a sound research program, but Stage 1 is **not currently a validated benchmark or submission-ready methods paper**. The current title has been narrowed to *Prospective Forecasting of Computer Science Research Breakthroughs: Protocol Design and a Pilot Event Registry*. Broad novelty for a scientific forecasting benchmark is contradicted by PreScience; the closest question-level historical/ prospective protocol is the same project's astronomy work. A domain-specific CS extension might become useful, but the ten-record registry, without outcome labels or independent event review, does not establish a publishable contribution. See `CS_FORECASTING_PRIOR_ART_AND_NOVELTY_AUDIT.md` and `CS_FORECASTING_SUBMISSION_READINESS.md`.

## Stage 1 — possible protocol/resource contribution (not yet achieved)

Keep only as a future objective. A defensible contribution must identify a specific methods advance beyond the astronomy historical-backtesting protocol and PreScience; publish complete source-only eligibility and selection flow; obtain independent event-definition reviews; resolve the frozen ORS deadline defect through provenance without rewriting history; handle the original three's issue/horizon interval explicitly; add source version/content hashes where lawful; test the separate Target B rubric with blinded humans; audit licenses; and demonstrate practical use beyond its author. The current 10-item pilot is a development artifact, not a validated or representative benchmark.

Possible conditional scope fit:

- [Quantitative Science Studies](https://direct.mit.edu/qss): theoretical/empirical research about science and novel methods; data essential to findings should be openly shared with a persistent identifier. Domain application of existing methods needs a novel science-studies contribution.
- [Research Evaluation](https://academic.oup.com/rev/pages/author-guidelines): appropriate only if the central contribution advances research-evaluation theory/practice and makes that connection explicit.
- NeurIPS 2026 E&D accepted protocols/datasets but its May 4/6, 2026 deadlines have passed; it is not a current submission route. Its call emphasized meaningful contributions to AI evaluation and accessible documented artifacts.

These are scope checks, not recommendations to submit or claims of likely acceptance. Recheck official requirements if a future manuscript is ready.

## Stage 2 — predictive validity (future; not evaluated)

Question: do prospectively issued forecasts for Target A direct resolution/refutation or project-specific Target B evidence growth outperform simple point-in-time baselines? This must wait for mature, independently adjudicated outcomes. Requirements remain: target-specific labels, enough independent research families, chronology-preserving splits, untouched final test, source-only features, calibration only for actual probabilities, publication-volume/field-momentum/text baselines, proper scoring, cluster-aware uncertainty and power. Missing evidence stays censored; no probability or AUC is inferred from the current qualitative forecasts.

Paper 3 attention/resolution/premise-revision records, this program's Target B, and Paper 4 action-utility outcomes remain distinct. Stage 2 cannot silently pool them.

## Governance and stop rules

No outcome monitoring, worker, scheduled job, model fitting, forecast submission, or OpenFindings publication is authorized here. Preserve all frozen records. If the Stage 1 novelty delta and event-quality gates cannot be established, do not submit a paper merely because the two-stage plan was previously proposed. External independent review is still required.
