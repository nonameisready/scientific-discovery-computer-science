# Prior-art and novelty audit: prospective CS research forecasting

**Audit date:** 2026-10-17. **Scope:** primary-source and official-program comparison; not an exhaustive systematic review. No novelty/firstness claim is made.

## Verdict

The broad contribution “a scientific forecasting benchmark/framework” is not novel: PreScience was publicly available by February 2026 and explicitly presents a scientific forecasting dataset/benchmark, temporally aligned data, multiple forecasting tasks, baselines, evaluation, and released artifacts. The narrower target here—future resolution/refutation of a source-frozen, theorem-level CS question—is distinguishable from PreScience’s paper-anchored contribution, collaborator, citation, and aggregate topic tasks. But this project has only ten selected qualitative events, no matured outcomes, no independent event reviewers, and no predictive comparison. Distinct target definition alone does not establish a sufficiently original or useful benchmark contribution.

The closest method prior is the same project's Mao (2026) historical astronomy backtesting paper. It already freezes questions and corpus cutoffs, uses temporally isolated later evidence, labels answered/partial/independently-posed/ignored and premise support/refutation, compares baselines, studies inter-rater agreement, and releases 200 prospective questions. The CS registry may be a domain adaptation, but the current work does not demonstrate an independent methodological advance beyond that protocol. This self-overlap must be disclosed and an explicit contribution delta demonstrated before submission.

## Established design practices versus possible incremental contribution

Established practices reflected here include prospective preregistration, point-in-time feature cutoffs, exact endpoint definitions, temporal splits, group/family blocking, proper scoring, censoring, source provenance, double adjudication, and explicit separation of outcome from publication attention. The current artifacts combine these ideas in a CS theorem-event registry. No new statistical estimator, adjudication theory, event ontology validated by users, new benchmark evaluation result, or predictive result was established in this cycle.

Potentially differentiating target: an event-level dataset of exact open theoretical/algorithmic CS questions with source-version cutoffs, theorem-scope matching, counterexample/refutation outcomes, and evidence-trajectory dimensions. To warrant a publication claim, it still needs (i) a nontrivial, transparent, reproducible sampling frame; (ii) source-complete inclusion/exclusion records; (iii) domain-expert review of every event; (iv) reliable independent outcome adjudication; (v) licensing/provenance and archived-version commitments; (vi) enough events to demonstrate coverage and benchmark utility; and (vii) a clear methodological delta against Mao 2026 and PreScience. No claim that the ten-item set meets these conditions is justified.

## Priority and publication assessment

`NOVELTY_NOT_ESTABLISHED`; broad benchmark firstness is contradicted by prior art. A future narrowly scoped CS theorem-question resource could be incrementally useful, but publication value is currently low/uncertain as a standalone methods paper. The two-paper sequence remains a defensible *program plan*, not an established two-paper publication result: Stage 1 must itself make a new, validated contribution; Stage 2 must await labels and test performance. A safer current title is “Protocol Design and a Pilot Event Registry,” not “Benchmark” unless the benchmark-quality bar is met.
