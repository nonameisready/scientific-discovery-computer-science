## What can and cannot be reconstructed

The artifact names the seven inclusions and the seven excluded/rejected leads. The exclusions are mostly described as already solved at a broad endpoint or insufficiently stable semantics; the list includes 4-block IP FPT, QMC linear-sample, minimum-dilation spanning tree, unit-disk diameter, orthogonal polygon square covering, point-algebra MinCSP, and amnesiac flooding. This supports a partial narrative of the screen.

The artifacts do **not** provide a complete 14-row table containing candidate ID, first discovery source/date, database/query, exact query string, source versions inspected, inclusion criteria, reason for inclusion/exclusion, reviewer, and deduplication links. Nor do they show a balanced candidate pool from which the ten frozen questions were sampled. In particular, selected questions are disproportionately theory milestones with identifiable theorem endpoints; empirically grounded CS subfields are not represented comparably. Therefore the selection process is not independently reproducible from the current record.

## Bias and dependence

This is a purposeful convenience sample generated by a research-radar workflow, not a random or stratified sample of open CS questions. Searchability, author-stated open problems, the researcher's familiarity, theorem-style falsifiability, and horizon plausibility all influence inclusion. Those are acceptable for a pilot registry but invalidate claims of representativeness or population prevalence.

Events differ in solution latency, theorem difficulty, base rates, and binary-event observability. The family map correctly links dynamic matching and ORS, but likely does not capture every shared author, technique, or dependence. Effective independent sample size is therefore at most the family count and may be lower. With no labels, there is no basis for power calculation from observed outcomes or for claims that the pilot has adequate positive/negative balance.

## Timing and frozen-date integrity

The first three records are issued on 2026-10-17 but their snapshot metadata has nominal observation start 2027-01-01, while their exact events say “by deadline.” They remain unchanged. A post-issue result before 2027 must not be hidden or treated as pre-issue; future reporting should show issue-to-2026-12-31 as an explicit early interval and separately describe the nominal horizon. The ORS event has a distinct deadline disagreement and is not safely adjudicable until its preregistration source is resolved.

## Recommendation for any future cohort (not an automatic task)

Before new forecasts are frozen, define the sampling frame, search databases and time, deduplication keys, field strata, source-only eligibility criteria, event feasibility gates, family grouping, and complete row-level inclusion/exclusion log. Have domain experts review event semantics before issuance, not after outcomes. Publish the flow diagram and the underlying candidate table. Do not backfill these requirements by inventing records for this already frozen cohort.

## Decision

The existing cohort is a transparent-enough pilot to inspect at record level but does not have an auditable sampling denominator or reproducible complete screening flow. No inferential or representative-benchmark interpretation is supported. Preserve all frozen records and treat this as a documented selection limitation, not a negative scientific result.
