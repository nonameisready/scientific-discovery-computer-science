# Scientific Discovery 3.0 — computational opportunity screen

**Decision: NO-GO for selecting a new research task in this bounded cycle.** Eight concrete opportunities across inference, AI retrieval, database algorithms, ANN, dynamic graphs, and query optimization were screened against primary papers. Each admits an explicit mechanism to test, but the likely contribution either overlaps a recent method that already targets the proposed objective, lacks an exact source-grounded gap, or needs empirical resources and a strong baseline that this assignment explicitly does not authorize. No algorithm was implemented, no benchmark was run, and no performance claim is made.

## 1. Research-scope policy and lessons from the prior campaign

Computer science capability is the campaign's primary target. Mathematical work is in scope only when it yields an identifiable improvement to an algorithm, data structure, complexity bound, or executable system. Validity of a proof is not evidence of computational impact by itself.

The prior work is retained as mathematically or experimentally valid within its scope: exact Zero Extension reductions for trees/cacti and contraction-only examples; a parallel token-swapping 2-approximation for double-stars plus h-dependent bounds; and the stated profile-complexity reconstruction and stress tests. These did not establish an unrestricted algorithmic improvement. Recurring failure modes were: (i) beginning from large open conjectures without a tractable first contribution; (ii) proving small restricted cases with no meaningful capability gain; (iii) recasting a full conjecture as an “intermediate” lemma equivalent to it; (iv) repeating audits after a proof route had already been closed; and (v) mistaking a completed artifact or task for scientific discovery. The proof-mechanism cycle further showed that transferring a method requires matching its preserved semantics—not just matching the vocabulary of “states” or “representatives.”

No prior result is reopened here. The supervisor, experimental controls, Paper 3/4, historical experiment records, and user-dirty queue/state files are outside this edit.

### Evidence and scope notes

Primary sources checked include H2O and SnapKV, OPT-Tree, Sequoia and SMART, Preble, TaiChi, the 2026 heavy-light IVM paper, RaBitQ and its extension, Bhore–Chan, CardOOD, Adaptive-k, MBA-RAG, and SARA. The source URLs and the exact reported claims are in the JSON artifact. “No gap found” means only that this bounded targeted search did not isolate one; it is not proof that a field has no open problems.

The strongest candidate by tractability and exact semantics is C5, but it does not yet pass the admission gate. The ACM primary PDF endpoint returned HTTP 403 in this session; the high-level problem scope is visible in the official metadata/abstract, while detailed claims about maintenance width and threshold choice were only visible in an accessible paper-text mirror and have **not** been independently reconstructed from the primary full text. Its proposed adaptive-threshold portfolio could be routine or already implied by threshold-selection and update-dependent maintenance literature. Until the primary theorem is obtained and a nontrivial dynamic-skew regime is isolated, selecting it would manufacture novelty. The cycle therefore selects **no** candidate and creates no algorithm-design report.

## 3. Concrete mechanism screen and independent challenge

Every row specifies a proposed operation rather than an aspirational system. The strongest apparent mechanism, C5, is:

* **Input:** a fixed conjunctive query hypergraph, a stream of single-tuple updates, current relation/key frequencies, and a threshold portfolio `T={1,2,4,...,2^⌈log N⌉}`.
* **State:** exact materialized delta views for a selected threshold, plus (only if space permits) metadata identifying heavy/light key partitions and migration progress.
* **Proposed operation:** maintain the currently selected heavy-light decomposition; update empirical degree/skew summaries; switch threshold only when a hysteresis condition predicts that the maintenance-width reduction over a bounded horizon exceeds the exact view-migration cost; migrate in the background while queries continue to use a versioned, exact old view until atomic publication of the new one.
* **Output/correctness:** exact query answers throughout, with constant-delay enumeration after each committed update. No approximation of query output is allowed.
* **Cost target:** bound update work plus migration, retained view space, and enumeration delay. A plausible theorem would compare the adaptive total cost over a sequence against the best single fixed threshold in hindsight, with an additive initialization/migration term. No such theorem is proved here.
* **Fast falsifier:** a two-relation join with one key's frequency alternating around a threshold, forcing repeated migrations; calculate whether hysteresis can simultaneously avoid thrashing and track a sudden sustained skew shift.

This mechanism does **not** pass selection: the benchmark objective and online comparator need a prior-art theorem audit, the migration protocol may duplicate a known dynamic threshold technique, and exact constant-delay behavior during reclassification is an unresolved correctness obligation. No implementation or experiment is authorized or performed. The other candidates' input/state/operation/output/correctness/cost/failure specifications are captured in structured form in the companion JSON.

Independent review: the most likely hidden costs are duplicated views (space), update amplification during threshold migration, buffering/latency while rebuilding, and the possibility that query output itself changes too much for constant update work. An adversary can oscillate frequency distributions across a decision boundary. A fair future evaluation would include fixed-threshold optimum selected with hindsight only as an oracle upper bound—not a deployable baseline—and compare to the best *causal* static threshold plus the original paper's optimized threshold. This cycle did not establish that the proposed policy improves either baseline.

## 4. Lightweight reusable CS-discovery workflow

1. **Literature/novelty gate:** exact input/output objective, strongest primary-source theorem/code, forward search through cutoff, and a sentence identifying what is not solved. Reject if the proposed rule is already present.
2. **Algorithm-specification gate:** write input, decision-time information, maintained state, one-operation pseudocode, output, invariants, and failure cases before implementation.
3. **Semantics/correctness gate:** state exact result semantics; prove invariants or use a differential oracle. For approximate methods, freeze the error/recall/quality definition.
4. **Cost gate:** account for preprocessing, update, query, memory, data movement, synchronization, retries, energy where measurable, and amortization. Do not count one resource while hiding another.
5. **Adversarial gate:** construct a minimal case that should break the mechanism and a workload-shift case; distinguish falsifying a design from disproving a broader problem.
6. **Baseline gate:** select the strongest same-semantics implementation and simple baselines; match hardware, data, accuracy, budgets, and tuning opportunity.
7. **Authorization gate:** experiments require a frozen hypothesis, versions, commands, seeds, metrics, uncertainty unit, budget, and explicit currently valid authorization. No implicit renewal.
8. **Reproducibility/reporting gate:** preserve raw outputs and failures, independent validation, report negatives, update registry/checkpoint, and make no claim beyond executed evidence.

This is a lightweight checklist, not a new software platform.

## 5. Final decision

**NO-GO.** No candidate has all of (i) source-isolated novelty, (ii) a concrete correctness-preserving mechanism, and (iii) a feasible validation route inside the current no-experiment authorization. C5 is the strongest research lead but remains unselected. The exact next scientific decision—only after a separate future assignment—is whether the 2026 heavy-light IVM algorithm and adjacent update-dependent maintenance work leave an online skew-adaptation theorem that is not already implied by their maintenance-width threshold optimization. If not, close C5 without implementing it.

No benchmark, dataset evaluation, inference, parameter search, simulation, or worker was run. Paper 3 and Paper 4 were not changed. No task was added to the queue and no experimental budget was altered.
